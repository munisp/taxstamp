# Compliance Research Sources

## Nigeria Data Protection Commission

The Nigeria Data Protection Commission website identifies the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 as the governing framework and exposes controller/processor registration, audit filing, audited-firm listings, and privacy-breach reporting services. Its data-subject-rights summary includes information, access, rectification, objection, restriction, portability, erasure, complaint, and automated-decision rights. These sources establish the audit criteria for privacy notice, rights-handling, breach response, data-controller/processor classification, registration, and audit evidence. [1]

## Central Bank of Nigeria Payments System Supervision

The CBN Payments System Supervision page states a mandate to promote payment-system soundness and safety through rules and guidelines, strong internal controls, transparency and accountability in fintechs, effective supervision, and monitoring capable of early-warning signals. It describes the CBN as the main payment-system regulator and identifies banks, payment-service providers, switches and related institutions as key participants. These materials support the technical audit criteria for access control, monitoring, operational resilience, transaction integrity, evidence retention, and partner oversight. Whether the Taxstamp operator itself is directly subject to a particular CBN rule depends on its licence, payment role, and contractual structure. [2]

[2]: https://www.cbn.gov.ng/PaymentsSystem/ "CBN Payments System Supervision"

## NDPC General Application and Implementation Directive

The official NDP Act General Application and Implementation Directive (GAID) 2025 states that the older NDPR 2019 is no longer the legal instrument used to regulate privacy after GAID’s issuance, subject to the NDP Act’s transitional treatment. Its Article 7 lists relevant controller/processor measures: registration where classified as of major importance; an NDP Act compliance audit within 15 months of commencement and annually thereafter; compliance-audit returns for specified major-importance classifications; compliance schedules; semi-annual data-protection reports; monitoring, evaluation and maintenance of a confidentiality, integrity and availability security system; privacy training; a DPO for controllers/processors of major importance; transparent published privacy policies and notices; DPIAs when required; and notification of personal-data breaches to the Commission within 72 hours of awareness. The Directive’s contents also address data-processing agreements, data-subject rights, cross-border transfers, and evidence of compliance. [3]
